Hardball Tactics

Sometimes the other side is deliberately trying to manipulate the negotiation.

The important thing is recognizing the move.

⸻

Excessive anchoring

They start with an extreme demand.

Response: don’t react emotionally. Ask for justification.

⸻

Negotiating against yourself

They make a proposal.

I reject it.

They say nothing.

And I immediately improve my own offer.

Problem: I have just negotiated against myself.

⸻

Red herring

They introduce an issue that isn’t actually important to them.

I spend energy negotiating it.

They eventually “concede” it.

They may have obtained something more important in exchange.

⸻

Big concession for a small concession

They give me something that costs them very little but ask for something valuable in return.

Always ask:

“What is this worth to them?”

not just:

“What is this worth to me?”

⸻

Linkage

They connect two issues:

“If you want X, you need to give us Y.”

This can be legitimate, but it can also be a pressure tactic.

⸻

Good cop / bad cop

One person appears reasonable.

The other appears impossible.

The “reasonable” person then becomes the person I want to make a deal with.

⸻

Hands tied

“I would love to accept, but my boss won’t allow it.”

Maybe true.

Maybe not.

⸻

Ultimatum

“Take it or leave it.”

The important question is whether they actually have the power and willingness to walk away.

⸻

Cherry on the cake

After the main agreement appears complete:

“One last little thing…”

The request may be small but strategically timed.

⸻

Memory hook

Don’t fight the tactic. Identify the tactic.

Once I recognize what is happening, I have more control over my response.
